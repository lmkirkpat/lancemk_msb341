## 2. Proposed edits to the guide

Wording is a draft in the guide's own style: start from a real event, no feature descriptions
in questions, no praise.

### 2a. Section 1 (warm-up): add

- "Which of those do you use most days, and which one did you open this week?" (Anchors the
  later stories in a real week, and tells us whether After Effects is even in their week.)
- **Screen check:** "Is After Effects something you use? When did you last open it?" If no,
  the interview is still useful for beliefs 1, 2, 5, 12, 16, but mark that the pitch is about
  an app they don't use (the problem 008's notes named) and skip the panel session.

### 2b. Section 2: replace the switching question (belief 6)

Replace "When you used YouTube or a chatbot for it, where was it on your screen? What was it
like going back and forth?" with a neutral version that doesn't invite yes/no:

- "Walk me through the last time you did that. What was on your screen, and what did you click
  or switch to first?" Then: "What happened between the video or chat and the app?" Don't
  ask "does that bother you?" (008 shows it produced a ¼ answer).
- Add: "Did you ever lose your place, or have to redo something because of that?" (a cost, not
  a feeling).
- If they have a second monitor or phone, ask them to say where the help sat.

### 2c. Section 2: add a version question (belief 16)

Put this right after the "how did you figure it out" question, so it's before any pitch:

- "Thinking about that time: did the steps you found match what you saw on screen? Tell me
  about a time they didn't." Follow-ups: "What did you do?" "How long did that take?" "Which
  version of the app were you on?"
- Don't say "version" in the first question. If they bring up versions or old menus unprompted
  that's a stronger Did than a yes to a prompt; score it as such, and note which it was.
- If they say "no, it matched": ask for the last time it didn't. Record "none they could
  recall" as evidence against.

### 2d. Section 5 (learn vs. done): make it protected, and sharpen it (belief 7)

Section 5 was skipped in 008 and is the only route to belief 7. Move it to just after
section 3 so it can't be squeezed out, and add a ★ to the first question only if time is
short. Keep the two existing ★ questions and add:

- "Think of a project that's finished. Is there something in it you got done but still don't
  know how to do yourself? Tell me about it." (Finds the skipped learning.)
- "Have you gone back to it since? What made you, or what stopped you?" (The "after the
  deadline" question, split from the first so each gets an answer.)
- "Last time you did that thing, was there a time of year, a gap between projects, or a slow
  week when you tried to learn it properly?" This asks about timing without suggesting that
  a mentor should exist.
- Keep the deadline contrast: "the last time you were stuck right before a deadline, and the
  last time with no deadline." If they have no no-deadline story, that is a finding. Write it
  down.

### 2e. Section 6: split "Adobe AI" into two questions (belief 10, open Q2)

008 was ambiguous between generative features and the in-app assistant. Replace the single
question with a short sequence. Don't describe either feature:

1. "When you hear 'AI in Adobe apps,' what's the first thing you think of?" (Their word, not
   ours.)
2. "Have you used any AI feature built into [their Adobe app]? Open it and show me where it
   is. What did you ask or do?" For a remote call, ask them to share the screen or describe
   the panel or button. This sorts generative fill/extend from the Assistant panel for the
   notes.
3. "What did you do right after: keep it, redo it, or go somewhere else?" (The behavior.)
4. If they name only generative features: "Have you ever asked an Adobe app how to do
   something, in a panel or a chat inside the app?" Yes/no, then the story. If no, record
   "has not used the in-app assistant" and move on. Don't explain it.

Write up each answer with a label: **generative feature**, **in-app assistant**, **help
button or docs**, or **unclear**.

### 2f. Section 4: one concrete memory question (open Q1, belief 2)

The guide's four questions are good. Two additions:

- "The thing you looked up again: how long, in total, from realizing you'd forgotten to being
  back at work? What would you have done with the time?" (Puts a cost on one story.)
- Replace "Do you keep anything to remember what you've learned" with a show-me:
  "Can you show me where you'd look if you needed to redo that thing next month?" (008's old
  project files came out of an open version of this. A show-me is a Did even over a call.)

### 2g. Section 7 (reaction): keep, with two changes

- **Show-the-panel is the strongest evidence; make it the default for After Effects users.**
  See the recruiting plan below. Add a note to the guide that the panel session is logged as
  product use, separate from the interview, and the interviewer must say nothing while the
  learner does the "you do" step (see 3).
- Add the name check, as the very last item before the close, so it can't affect any answer:
  "Last thing: I've been calling this Knack. When you hear that, what does it make you think
  of? Anything else with that name?" Write down the first reaction word for word. This feeds
  `decisions/006` signal 2. Don't mention knack.com or the other products.

### 2h. Section 8 (close): move referrals ahead of everything in section 7

Three interviews lost the referral question when the recording stopped. Put the two close
questions after section 6 and before the pitch or the panel session. Ask: "Who else do you
know who has learned something new in a creative app recently?" Offer a way to pass it on
that isn't a name: "Would you forward them a message from me?" Names go in notes only as a
role, never in the repo.

### 2i. "Rules for myself": three additions

- **Ask for the screen first, not the story.** A show-me beats a told story, so ask for the
  actual app, panel or file whenever the story is about one.
- **Log the question you asked, not just the answer.** 007 and 008 lost questions in the
  transcript. After each interview, list each question that mattered in the write-up so the
  scoring can say whether I led.
- **Mark what kind of reaction you got.** An answer after the pitch is Weak (¼); say so in
  the table every time.

### 2j. Edits to "What these interviews need to answer now"

The six items stand. Change the order to match the evidence gaps: 3 (belief 7) first, then 4
(belief 6) and 2 (Adobe's assistant). Add item 7: **Does the version mismatch happen in After
Effects?** (belief 16). Add item 8: **What does the name bring to mind?** (`decisions/006`).

## 3. A target-market learner on the panel (belief 7, belief 15)

### What the session needs to show

The only evidence that can move belief 7 above Low, or belief 15's "does the work for you"
half, is behavior with the product from someone who isn't the builder. What counts, by the
rubric: what the person did in a real lesson, not what they said about it. Look for:

- Whether they do the "you do" step or ask the mentor to do the rest (the do-it-for-me test,
  belief 7).
- Whether they reach for another source, such as Adobe's assistant, YouTube or a chatbot
  (open Q2, SC-007 in the builder's own notes).
- Where they hesitate, what they ask, and whether the lesson's checks match what they see in
  AE (belief 15, "wrong" half).
- Whether the lesson's topic was something they wanted at that moment (belief 7: the moment).

### Who

Match the guide's screen, plus After Effects:

- Uses After Effects at least a little, now or in the past 3 months. Paid, class or job
  motion work all count; log which.
- Someone the builder doesn't know well. Four of eight were friends; 008 came cold.
- Best fit first: a working in-house or solo designer who has an animation task coming. A
  student in an After Effects class is a fit too (007 was one), but the builder is a student
  in the same setting, so a student recruited from the same department adds less than a
  stranger.
- Not someone who has watched the builder's demos.

### Where to find them (suggestions, none tried)

1. **In person, cold, as with 008.** The same method: notice After Effects or Premiere on a
   classmate's or colleague's screen and ask if they do creative work. This is the only
   recruiting method that has produced a stranger so far.
2. **Motion-class classmates and the department's other designers**, via a plain message to
   the class or office channel: "Looking for someone who uses After Effects to try a 30-minute
   lesson tool I built. I'll show you, you do the work." This will mostly reach people the
   builder knows a little, so log how well.
3. **Referrals from the interview round.** The guide change in 2h gives each interview a
   second source. Ask each After Effects user who they know.
4. **Public posts** (a student design group, a motion community) with the same short ask and
   no product description beyond one sentence. Slower; the people who answer self-select.
   Any post would carry the name question, so check the name-check note first.

Reaching five candidates should produce two or three sessions; I'm guessing, not citing a
response rate.

### Format: choose between two

Both are possible now. Which one to use is a builder decision.

| | A. On the builder's machine, the Mentor Practice comp | B. On the tester's machine, their own comp |
|---|---|---|
| What it proves | The panel teaches a stranger; the lesson works on a prepared comp | The panel works in a stranger's project, on their version of AE |
| Needs | Nothing new. The builder is at the machine. | The installer (spec 006), signed-off fresh-account runs (T017 to T019) and an access key for the tester (spec 006 Q2, pinned and not decided) |
| Risk | The builder is in the room: the "doesn't do it for you" check can be influenced; the comp is the builder's own | Blocked on Q2; first run of the installer in the wild |
| Evidence weight | Did (product use), but the comp is familiar to the builder, and it should be labeled as such | Strongest, and tests belief 16 (their AE version) at the same time |

Also: `CLAUDE.md` says a builder-only session isn't enough for belief 7, and the live
sittings so far have all been the builder's. Format A answers that, but only partly: the
comp is still one the builder prepared.

### Session outline (about 45 minutes)

1. **Interview first (20 min)**, using the edited guide, sections 1 to 5. No product talk.
2. **Referral and follow-up ask**, before the product.
3. **Panel session (15 to 20 min).** Say: "I'm going to open a lesson. I won't help; I'm
   watching what you do." Then say as little as possible. The lesson is whatever the mentor
   proposes in their comp (don't choose the skill for them). Record screen and audio with
   their OK, if possible.
4. **Short debrief (5 min), after the session:** what they expected, what they'd do next,
   whether they would have gone elsewhere. Anything said here is Weak, but it explains the
   behavior.
5. **Name question last.**

### What to log (write-up)

Use `000-interview-template.md`, plus a product-use block kept separate from the interview:

- Time in lesson, who was talking, number of times the interviewer spoke.
- Each time they asked the mentor to do it for them, or wanted to skip a step. Quote them.
- Each place they looked outside the panel (tab, phone, another AI).
- Each check the mentor made versus what AE shows (agreement table from `/usage-session`).
- Whether their stated deadline pressure that day was high, low or none.
- Hesitations over 10 seconds and what they said.

Anonymize as usual: roles only, no names, employers or emails in the notes or the file name.

### What would count as evidence against

Plan for it before the session. Examples that would lower belief 7's claim or belief 15's:

- They finish the lesson and say they'd have rather had it done (supports belief 7's
  "moment" claim, narrowly: it counts only if they say it *during* a real task).
- They don't ask for the demo to go further (against belief 15's "does the work" half).
- They use Adobe's assistant or a chatbot mid-lesson (open Q2, and belief 10).
- They finish and want to go on to the next skill without being led (a point for leading and
  memory, against the "mid-project they take the result" worry).

### Things I can't tell from the repo

- Whether format A on the builder's own machine can be run with the AE lock hooks in play
  (the live-sitting rules in `CLAUDE.md` say one sitting at a time).
- Whether the mentor's current opening would propose a lesson that fits a stranger's comp.
  That's a question for the next live check, not for this note.

## 4. Gaps this proposal leaves open

- None of the edits tests whether people would *choose* the mentor over Adobe's assistant
  (open Q2). Only a session where both are on the screen does. The panel session above can
  note whether they open the other.
- Pay is still all talk. A "what have you paid for" Did question is in the guide already; this
  round needs it asked, not just written.
- A stranger found cold may be an easier sample than a working professional. Row sources in
  the recruiting list are not ranked by evidence quality, only by what has worked.
