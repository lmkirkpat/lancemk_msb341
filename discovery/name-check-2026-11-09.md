# Name check: "Knack" (2026-11-09)

Prep for spec 006 T046. This is a desk search of the open web, not a legal clearance. I'm not
a lawyer, and nothing here is legal advice. The trademark data comes from third-party
aggregator sites (Justia, Markinton, TrademarkDir), not the USPTO's own record (TSDR). I could
not open the aggregator pages themselves (blocked or unreachable), so owners, classes and
statuses below are as shown in search snippets. Every row needs checking in TSDR before anyone
relies on it. **A professional trademark search is needed before a real launch.**

This note records what exists. It doesn't recommend a name; the builder decides.

## Against `decisions/006` › What would change our mind

First signal: "If a domain or trademark search finds a 'Knack' mark covering software,
education, or creative tools, then add a qualifier (Knack Studio) or rename before any public
launch."

**This search found live KNACK marks in software and education classes, so the first signal
looks met on the evidence below (unverified at the registry).** The second signal (interviewees
mention knack.com when they hear the name) hasn't been tested; no interview has said the name.
The third (five-second tagline test) hasn't been run either.

## How this reads against 006's signals

- **Signal 1 (marks covering software, education or creative tools):** looks met. Rows 1, 2 and
  4 are the ones to read at TSDR first. Row 2 is the closest, on both the word and the field.
- **Signal 2 (interviewees mention another product when they hear the name):** untested. Cheap
  to add to the next round (see `discovery/interview-prep-2026-11-09.md`).
- **Signal 3 (five-second tagline test):** untested.
- What the 006 text assumed: "different field, so no confusion for a creative user." That holds for
  row 1 only. Row 2 is a learning product for students, and row 3 is a "skills" product for
  Claude users, so the assumption is weaker than it was when it was written.

## Things I could not do

- Open the aggregator pages or TSDR, so owners, classes, statuses and the goods and services
  wording are unconfirmed. Snippets can lag or be wrong. One snippet gave two different
  owner locations for what may be the same company.
- Check international marks (EUIPO, WIPO, UK, Canada). A tester or buyer outside the US would
  need those.
- Check domains, social handles, or Adobe Exchange.
- Judge likelihood of confusion. That is a legal call that depends on goods, channels and the
  marks' strength.

## What a professional search would cover

A trademark attorney or a full search report would check the USPTO and international
registers, pending applications, state marks and common-law use (including the App Store
listing in row 2), the goods and services wording of rows 1, 2 and 4, and whether a qualifier
or a different mark would clear.

## For the builder (not a recommendation)

Questions that this raises for `decisions/006`, in the order they would matter:

1. Does the builder want TSDR checked on rows 1, 2 and 4 now (free, about an hour), or only at
   the professional search?
2. Is the first outside tester a "public launch" under 006? A private tester with an installer
   is much lower risk than a store listing, but 006 says to search before the installer ships
   to anyone else, and the installer task (T046) says before the first tester.
3. If 006's first signal counts as met, 006 already names the two paths: add a qualifier or
   rename. The data doesn't say which; row 2 suggests a qualifier alone may not clear a class
   9 or 41 mark.
