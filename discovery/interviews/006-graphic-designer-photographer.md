# Interview: professional graphic designer and photographer, Adobe daily user

**Date:** 2026-10-01
**How I found them:** friend (recorded with a transcription app; raw transcript not committed
because it contains names)
**Persona:** 1, the skill-builder (`discovery/personas.md`). First interviewee who works in
Adobe apps every day for paid work. Fits the target market (solo or in-house designer).

## What I asked / what happened

Used `bet-a-interview-guide.md`: past behavior first, pitch near the end. The transcript
lumps both speakers together and drops many of their short answers, so only clear lines are
quoted. Their reaction to the pitch itself is mostly lost.

- **Apps:** Lightroom for photos. Illustrator for logos and graphics for their internship.
  Photoshop because "it pulls everything in." InDesign: "I use it all the time." A little
  Premiere. Didn't know After Effects by name.
- **Last thing they learned:** redoing a clipping mask in Photoshop. "I just took a picture
  of all my layers and plugged it into AI, and then it just told me exactly what I need to do
  to switch around and like what cuts I need to make." Used Meta AI after ChatGPT stopped
  taking photo uploads on the free tier. Followed the steps themselves.
- **Why it worked:** "It was just very direct ... these are ways you could do it, but this is
  the best route. ... I could figure out how to do it, but that just told me exactly how to
  do it."
- **Other help:** YouTube, and a parent who is a graphic designer. AI is now first:
  you "don't have to like watch a video or like search Google."
- **Text-only drawback?** "No. As of now, no." Layer names made it feel "a little visual in
  its own sense." When I asked whether words alone would work in an app they know less (After
  Effects), the answer is garbled.
- **Settled for less?** No. Finished the way they wanted.
- **Adobe's built-in assistant (Photoshop):** didn't know it existed. "Maybe I just don't
  have the updated version."
- **Learn vs. done:** "I want to understand how to do the technique, but I don't care in what
  way it's done." If AI could make the finished piece, they'd still want to know how,
  "because I'd want it to be like my work. ... a design that I designed is being able to walk
  someone through my design and telling them exactly what I did."
- **Trust, after the pitch:** being built into the app makes them trust it *more*: "if
  anything's going to understand this software, it would be this." What would make them stop:
  if it "take[s] you on a different path" or "isn't direct." It needs to be "super clear and
  direct."
- **Would they pay?** "That would depend on my skill level." Basics no: relearning the
  clipping mask felt like "a basic thing ... I've done the clipping mask," so a quick free AI
  answer was enough. For going "deeper to understand like a different concept," yes.
- **How they'd want to pay:** sold as an add-on when buying the Adobe plan, maybe discounted
  at signup, "highly recommended in the program." Monthly, because "people feel safe with the
  monthly ... if I like it and I feel like I'm using it a lot, I'm going to keep using it."
- **Referrals:** none offered.

## What they actually do today

Adobe apps every day for paid design and photo work. When stuck: take a photo of the screen
(the Layers panel), give it to a free chatbot, follow the one recommended route. YouTube and
a designer parent before that. Hasn't noticed Adobe's own assistant.

## Strongest signal

**A working designer wants the lesson because the design has to be theirs.** Being able to
"walk someone through my design" is how they define authorship. That's a different reason
to learn than 003 and 005 had (or lacked), and it's the professional reason persona 1 is
built on.

Second: **skills fade even for professionals, but fading basics are cheap to fix.** They had
done clipping masks before and still had to look it up. That's the problem memory and practice
target, but they solved it in minutes with free AI and wouldn't pay for that. They'd pay only
for depth.

## Notes on how I ran it

- **Friend again**, so expect politeness, especially on the pitch and pay questions.
- **I praised them before the pitch** ("this is already going much better than the other
  interviews because you actually are in these softwares"). That likely warmed up the answers
  that followed.
- **The pitch was leading:** "it'll actively recommend steps to help you learn new techniques
  and to improve." Pay and trust answers came after it, so they're weaker.
- **The After Effects question was hypothetical and suggested its answer** ("would you need
  a visual?").
- **Memory and practice weren't asked as questions, on purpose.** The clipping-mask story
  answered it through behavior: the skill faded, and they went and fixed it rather than
  moving on. That's real demand for a fix. The fix they chose was relearning on demand (free
  AI, minutes), not avoiding the fade. So for basics, relearning on demand is what memory
  and practice compete with. Still open: whether relearning costs enough on deeper skills or
  an unfamiliar app for not forgetting to be worth more.
- **Not asked:** whether they've ever paid to learn; non-Adobe apps.
- **Good:** asked about Adobe's assistant before the pitch, and got a concrete recent story
  (the clipping mask) with the exact workaround.
