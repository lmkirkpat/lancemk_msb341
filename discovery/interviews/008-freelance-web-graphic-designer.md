# Interview: freelance web and graphic designer, on retainer with one company

**Date:** 2026-10-12
**How I found them:** in person, cold. A classmate I didn't know before; I saw Photoshop open
on their computer, asked if they did creative work, and interviewed them after they said yes.
Not a friend: the first stranger since the guide asked for them. (Recorded with a
transcription app; raw transcript not committed. Both speakers are merged in it, as in 007.)
**Persona:** 1, the skill-builder (`discovery/personas.md`), on the working end. Built a
company's website and is kept on for maintenance and updates. Photoshop for product images,
Illustrator for logos and icons, Adobe Animate for animation (less lately), Figma before. Does
not use After Effects. Met the guide's screen: paid creative work, and learned something new
(Photoshop's beta AI features) recently.

## What I asked / what happened

Used `bet-a-interview-guide.md` in order. The pitch came near the end ("So what I'm working on
is ... an AI-powered mentor inside of Creative Cloud apps"). Everything after it is Weak.

- **Apps and who pays:** Photoshop ("a lot of product images"), Illustrator ("a lot of logos
  and icons"), Animate, and Figma before ("not Figma anymore"). The employer pays for them.
- **Last thing they learned: Photoshop beta's AI features**, "selecting and then prompting to
  put something in." Learned from Adobe's preview shown before you open the app, "videos
  online" from Adobe and YouTube, and Adobe's documentation. Did not use another AI to learn
  it ("No, actually"). Still unsure: "I don't know if I know how to use it as well as I want
  to or if it just doesn't work that well."
- **Switching between sources:** asked "does that bother you?" (a yes/no I led): "yes, I wish
  it was all in one place. And honestly, I wish AI was so native in everything that when you
  have questions on a webpage like this, you can just type it." "It's frustrating trying to
  go through all these."
- **Something that looked wrong:** a logo three months ago. Exported as SVG, "there was like
  discoloration that I didn't understand because it wasn't showing within ... Illustrator
  itself." Found the fix with "just like normal Google." "Not super long, just a quick fix."
  (The question itself was lost from the transcript.)
- **Help that made it worse:** learning Photoshop "was brutal because most of the videos I
  watch ... was either extremely dated or wasn't relevant. ... versions of Photoshop that's
  like 2014 where nothing looks the same. ... I can't find any modern examples and so I'm just
  like stuck for a while."
- **AI as help:** "AI has definitely helped bridge the gap ... It's quicker, but it's not
  perfect." The limit: "sometimes AI pulls from so many sources that things get a little
  muddy ... some things will be labeled differently and it'll be labeled based on like a
  previous version or generation."
- **What they wish it did (before the pitch, unprompted):** "it would be nice is if it was
  almost doing it itself along with you. And so it was taking like screenshots or things like
  that to show you like, this is exactly what you're gonna do." And: "explanations of like, if
  you want this to look different, adjust this setting."
- **A skill they had to look up again:** exporting a certain file type in Photoshop, which
  they used to do "so often." Looked it up and "it turns out you can't do it," so they moved
  the work to Illustrator to export. "I tinkered around with for a while trying to figure out
  why it was wrong. Probably took me like 35 minutes." This is less a faded skill than not
  knowing why something failed (or the app changed).
- **Keeping what they learned:** "No, I don't think so. The only thing that I would say I
  keep to remind myself is like previous work. To where I can go in and see what I did."
- **Photoshop's beta AI in their work:** extended a canvas and had it fill in their own
  patterns. "Doesn't work very well." Deleted the results "because it was so bad." Tried
  "many different types of prompts," asked Claude what prompt to give Photoshop, and ended up
  "putting the image in like ChatGPT and saying ... extend this image because it's so much
  easier."
- **Pitch** (close to the guide's script, memory included): reads your comp, picks something
  to teach, shows you once, you do the rest while it checks and coaches, remembers what
  you've learned, won't do the work for you.
- **First reaction:** "I think it's a great idea, something I wish I had learning because it's
  still not easy. I don't think Adobe's documentation and helps ... [are] near relevant
  enough." "A lot of things I do are project-based, so I learn only the things that I need to
  complete a project. ... If I could tell it what I want to accomplish at the end of it and it
  ... teaches me things relevant to that, especially helpful."
- **In Photoshop, next to Photoshop's assistant?** (question garbled in the transcript)
  "I don't like Photoshop assistance. ... I don't find it super helpful." Unclear whether
  they mean the AI Assistant panel or the generative features above.
- **Who pays:** "Probably I would want the employer to do it, but I think any freelance person
  would pay for that too."
- **Not asked:** referrals, follow-up (transcript ends at the pay answer).

## What they actually do today

Adobe's own previews and docs plus YouTube to learn new features; Google for quick fixes; a
chatbot (ChatGPT, Claude) when it gets the result faster than Adobe's in-app AI. Keeps no
notes; goes back to their own old project files to see how they did something. Learns only
what the current project needs.

## Strongest signal

**Generic help is often for the wrong version, and they asked for help that shows it on their
own screen.** Old YouTube videos ("Photoshop that's like 2014 where nothing looks the same")
and AI steps "labeled based on like a previous version" both left them stuck. Before hearing
the pitch they described what they wanted: something "doing it itself along with you ...
taking like screenshots ... this is exactly what you're gonna do," plus why a setting matters.
A mentor that reads the live app gets the version right by design. No earlier interview named
version mismatch.

Second: **when Adobe's in-app AI failed, they left the app for a chatbot.** Many prompts, bad
results, deleted, then ChatGPT in another tab "because it's so much easier." Being in the app
didn't earn any loyalty once the answer was worse (belief 6, belief 15).

Third: **their memory is their old files.** No notes, no bookmarks: "previous work ... where I
can go in and see what I did." That's what a learner record competes with.

## Notes on how I ran it

- **A stranger, found cold.** Spotting Photoshop on a classmate's screen and asking worked,
  and it's repeatable. Less politeness risk than 001–007's friends, though "great idea" after
  the pitch still scores ¼.
- **Followed the guide's order and kept the pitch to the end.** No praise, and the pitch
  included memory this time (the 007 gap). On pay I didn't add my own view. Both good.
- **Led the switching question:** "does that bother you?" invites a yes. Belief 6 scores ¼
  for it. Next time: "what was going back and forth like?"
- **Asked "how long did that take?"** and got 35 minutes for the export story. Keep doing it.
- **Skipped section 5 (learn vs. done, deadline vs. none)** and the ★ "have you gone back to
  learn after a project" question. Belief 7 got only a post-pitch line.
- **Skipped "was there a time getting a skill back cost you more than a few minutes?"**
  (open Q1). The export story half-answered it by accident.
- **Didn't pin down which Adobe AI they meant.** Generative fill/extend versus the AI
  Assistant panel are different products; belief 10 and open Q2 need the second.
- **Didn't ask the post-pitch tie-back** ("earlier you said ... would this have changed
  that?"), the Adobe-vs-mentor question, or about Figma (open Q3).
- **Referrals and follow-up not asked**: the transcript ends at the pay answer again. The
  guide says ask for referrals before the last question; I didn't.
- **Speakers still merged** in the transcript, and the "something looked wrong" question is
  missing. Say who's talking at the start.
- **Fit:** not an After Effects user, so the pitch was about a product for an app they don't
  use. Their reaction is to the idea, not to something they'd open.
