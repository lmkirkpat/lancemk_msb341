# Interview: student photographer, social media specialist, videographer, and animator

**Date:** 2026-10-07
**How I found them:** friend (recorded with a transcription app; raw transcript not committed
because it contains names)
**Persona:** 1, the skill-builder (`discovery/personas.md`), on the student end. Took a
graduate-level motion graphics class in After Effects two semesters ago and has since left
that degree. Uses apps from several companies.

## What I asked / what happened

Used `bet-a-interview-guide.md`: past behavior first, pitch near the end. The transcript
merges both speakers and the ending is cut off, so only clear lines are quoted.

- **Apps:** Lightroom "religiously for like actual shoots." CapCut for video editing.
  Procreate for drawing (and class notes). Blender for animation. DaVinci Resolve before.
  After Effects for the motion graphics class. Photoshop and Illustrator in a design class.
  Tezza, a phone photo app, because it has the HSL sliders. Six or more companies.
- **Skilled by feel, not by name:** couldn't name HSL ("I know it started with an H"). "I
  don't know anything about — I just know where to find it and how to change it. ... I just
  kind of like slide it to what looks good." Their photo editing is good.
- **Last hard thing they learned: After Effects**, in a 500-level class they joined as an
  add-on. "I was trying to like keep myself from drowning." "Everything is like hidden," with
  shortcuts "that you have to do for everything."
- **Help from a person went badly:** they asked the classmate next to them what the last step
  was, and "instead of just like telling you what the last step was ... he just took my
  keyboard and like started doing all this stuff."
- **Memorized shortcuts don't stick:** a design class tested shortcuts in the testing center.
  "It's horrible, and then I forget them the day after."
- **How they got unstuck:** "YouTube videos, retracking steps." The teacher's own tutorial
  videos were the most useful. Finding a matching video took "hours."
- **The real cost was finding the mistake, not the video:** "you'll miss a step and then 5
  hours later in your project you'll be like, why is this messed up? And it's just because
  this one step is janky at the beginning." "I think it maybe took more time to find the
  error than to find a video that would help."
- **Settled for less?** "Yeah, definitely." Partly the program, partly "my laziness." "I have
  like high hopes for certain projects. But then I was like, I don't know if I have the
  capabilities to match how I want it to look."
- **AI for learning:** barely, maybe "where's this shortcut?" in Premiere or After Effects.
  A later teacher banned it: "if you use any AI, like, you will get kicked out of class and
  like reported. So I was like, yeah, I'm not even gonna try." Another teacher banned
  Photoshop's generative fill during the exam.
- **Learn vs. done:** "I feel like I'm bad in wanting to just get past it. But ... I know it
  would benefit me if I learned how to do it correctly." Mid-project it's clear: "If I've done
  4 hours on it, I don't want to do it."
- **If AI could make it, would they still want to know how?** "Yes, I just don't think that
  that's like your art. ... If AI makes it, it's not yours." Long story about a friend's
  AI-made song: "it was a good song. ... But there was no effort behind it." "The background
  and the story behind it is not there. And that's ... what makes it a piece."
- **First reaction to the pitch:** "I think that's a good idea." Compared it to Adobe's help
  button: "how helpful is it really? ... I don't know if I've ever used it and that's actually
  helped me." Framed it as "more of a tool versus like, write my paper for me."
- **Would it have helped with After Effects?** "It would have sped up the process a little
  bit," and "it'd be easier to spot the problem ... or even ask it, be like, what's the
  problem here? Why does it look like this?" Then find a video or ask how to fix it.
- **What would make them stop:** "If it was wrong." "If I have to ask it multiple times and
  it's giving me not what I want." "Or if it does it for me. ... if it starts like filling in
  stuff."
- **On demo-once:** when I said the mentor demonstrates once and refuses to do the rest:
  "That's cool." Then asked, unprompted, whether a tool like this would "dampen their
  ability" to know After Effects compared with someone who learned without it.
- **Would they pay?** "I don't know." Suggested a student price and a professional price:
  "students would get it if they were like, oh, student price, this is a safe spot." Doubted
  professionals would use it "because they've already learned," then changed their mind after
  I described my own case (see notes).
- **How to pay:** subscription, yearly like Adobe: "it feels like it's a long time before you
  have to renew it. So I guess that's a subscription, but it feels like a one-time."
- **Referrals:** not asked (transcript cuts off at the close).

## What they actually do today

Not in After Effects now. When they were: the teacher's tutorial videos, then YouTube, then
retracing their own steps to find where it broke. Almost no AI, partly because a teacher
banned it. Lightroom and CapCut by feel for regular photo and video work.

## Strongest signal

**Their biggest time sink was finding the mistake, not finding the instructions.** A step
missed early broke the project hours later, and tracking it down took longer than finding a
video. You can't search YouTube until you know what went wrong. That's the diagnosis job
(reading the project and saying what's wrong), and it's what they asked for first when they
imagined using it: "what's the problem here? Why does it look like this?"

Second: **they value authorship but take the shortcut mid-project.** "If AI makes it, it's
not yours" sits next to "If I've done 4 hours on it, I don't want to do it." The same person
wants both, depending on the moment. So whether people want the lesson may depend on the
moment more than on the person.

Third: **the trust-breaker in a human helper and in an AI is the same: doing it for you.**
The classmate who took the keyboard, and "if it does it for me ... starts filling in stuff."

## Notes on how I ran it

- **Friend again**, and I praised their photo editing early ("you edit photos well"). Expect
  politeness on the pitch and pay questions.
- **I shared my own experience during the pay question.** They doubted professionals would
  use it; I said I still learn new things in Illustrator every day and am learning After
  Effects now; they answered "that like made me change my mind." Their "professionals would
  use it" answer is mine, not theirs.
- **The pitch left out memory.** It came up only partly, when I answered their "dampen their
  ability" question ("it'll show you and then help you practice"). The question about a
  learning record or practice changing the choice over Adobe's assistant wasn't asked.
- **Adobe's assistant wasn't asked about directly.** They brought up the help button and
  generative fill on their own, but not the After Effects assistant.
- **Not asked:** ever paid to learn; how they learn Blender or Resolve compared with Adobe
  apps; a skill they've lost (the shortcut tests answered part of it); referrals; follow-up.
- **Good:** got a concrete past story (the After Effects class) with time costs ("hours," "5
  hours later"), and the learn-vs-done answer came before the pitch.
