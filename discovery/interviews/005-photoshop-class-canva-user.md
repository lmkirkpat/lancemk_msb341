# Interview: learned Photoshop basics in a class, now uses Canva now and then

**Date:** 2026-09-28
**How I found them:** friend, existing contact (recorded with a transcription app; raw
transcript not committed because it contains names)
**Persona:** closest to 2, the occasional user (`discovery/personas.md`). Doesn't fit the
guide's "learned a new app in the past 6 months" screen: their Photoshop learning was
before AI tools and they rarely use it now.

## What I asked / what happened

Used `bet-a-interview-guide.md`: past behavior first, pitch near the end. Parts of the
transcript are garbled, so only clear lines are quoted.

- **Apps:** Photoshop is "the biggest one" they've used, for photo editing, "but I don't use
  it as much anymore." Has used Canva's AI a little since.
- **How they learned Photoshop:** a class. Mostly color correction, plus some cutting out
  and blending. "Our teacher would give us like the rundown of it, but I mostly just used it
  through like trial and error."
- **When stuck:** "I'd either talk to my teacher or ... a lot of times I would like YouTube
  it or Google it."
- **Did they settle for less?** "For the most part, I was able to pull off what I wanted, but
  I also think that I was never really going for like super intense projects. Maybe if I
  would have stuck with it longer, I probably would have needed either like a lot more
  guidance from my teacher or something different." One garbled line follows that may say
  they were never really satisfied with where their tips came from. Too unclear to count.
- **AI so far:** "that was pre-AI." Has used Canva's AI "to like do the task for me." Nothing
  more than that.
- **Learn vs. done (hypothetical):** would still want to know how to do it themselves. "I like
  to be able to [have it] teach me how to do something for myself going forward."
- **Reaction to the pitch:** "That's super cool. I really like that, actually." Would have
  used it while learning Photoshop: "Totally." Likes AI "used as a tool, not just like
  replacing thinking."
- **Compared with other help:** an in-person teacher still wins ("I do learn better from like
  in-person things"). The mentor would beat YouTube because it's "more personalized than like
  trying to find like 4 different YouTube videos that are helping me with my specific
  problem."
- **Memory across sessions** (I described it, they reacted): "That's so epic."
- **Would it make them more likely to learn a professional app for their job?** "Yeah, I think
  so."

## What they actually do today

Very little creative software. Canva with its AI doing the task for them. When they did
learn Photoshop: a teacher's overview, trial and error, then the teacher, YouTube, or Google
when stuck.

## Strongest signal

**What they said and what they did point different ways.** They say they'd want AI to teach
them, but the one time they used AI in a creative app (Canva), they had it do the task. That
matches belief 7: people value learning in principle and take the result in practice.

The clearest comparison: **a real teacher is the best help they've had, and YouTube is the
worst of the options** because you have to piece together "4 different YouTube videos" for
one problem. Interview 003 also leaned on a person (an in-house editor).

## Notes on how I ran it

- **Weak evidence overall.** A friend, a lapsed and occasional user, and most answers after
  the warm-up were to hypotheticals ("if something like an AI could...", "would you have
  used...", "would it make you more inclined..."). The guide's rule is to ask about what
  they've actually done. Friends are the most likely to be polite.
- I described memory across sessions myself before they reacted, so "That's so epic" says
  little.
- Didn't use most of the new guide questions: Adobe's built-in assistant, who would pay,
  lost skills (they've clearly lost Photoshop, which was worth asking about), or what they
  needed to *see* rather than read.
- Good: the question about whether YouTube or the teacher got them what they wanted drew
  an honest "I was never really going for super intense projects."
