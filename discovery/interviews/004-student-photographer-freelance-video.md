# Interview: student, photographer for their own YouTube channel, freelance video editor

**Date:** 2026-09-28
**How I found them:** TODO (recorded with a transcription app; raw transcript not committed
because it contains names)
**Persona:** closest to 1, the skill-builder (`discovery/personas.md`), but with a firm "I
wouldn't pay"

## What I asked / what happened

Used `bet-a-interview-guide.md`: past behavior first, then pitched the idea near the end.
Parts of the transcript are garbled or repeated, so only clear lines are quoted.

- **Uses several apps from different companies:** Lightroom (photos for their YouTube
  channel), Photoshop now and then (fixing a photo, "somebody's eyes are closed"), Final Cut
  Pro (freelance video business), Snapseed (Instagram photos), and ChatGPT (school and job
  hunting). First interviewee who regularly uses creative apps from more than one company.
- **How they learned Lightroom:** "a lot of back and forth between the Lightroom window on my
  computer and ChatGPT." Described the goal in plain words, sometimes uploading the photo:
  "hey, the sky on my photo is really blown out. How can I fix it?" That's how they found
  tools like masking.
- **It carried over:** "I haven't been using chat as much recently when I use Lightroom
  because I've picked up on like my normal routines."
- **How they learn new things now:** Instagram reels from photographers who share editing
  tips, saved to a folder "that I don't always look back on, but occasionally I'll pull them
  up and try and copy their editing styles."
- **For a harder app (Photoshop), they'd pick YouTube over chat:** chat "will give me steps for
  simple tasks, but with something like Photoshop that's more complex, I would rather see a
  mouse on a screen moving and showing me where to go and how to do it than having to read
  instructions."
- **Chains the two:** "I might start with chat and then if I still don't get it, I have chat
  give me a YouTube link."
- **Has never paid to learn.** Their parents bought them presets at 15, which they never used.
  "I couldn't justify paying for something like that when there's just so many tools with AI
  and YouTube... I would rather do it myself and find my own way."
- **Reaction to the pitch:** "That is brilliant." "I would use it." Their first question was
  "Would it cost money?" Suggested, without prompting, that Adobe could bundle it into a
  premium or "beginner package."
- **Their own idea:** let creators sell their techniques like presets, as demos inside the
  tool. "There's some photographers that I'm like, I love their editing. I want to learn how
  to edit like them." Presets only really exist in Lightroom. A technique you can share would
  bring the idea to apps where presets don't work.

## What they actually do today

ChatGPT in plain words (sometimes with the photo) to learn the basics of an app. YouTube
when the task is complex enough that reading steps isn't enough, often reached through a link
ChatGPT gives them. Saved Instagram reels to copy a specific creator's look. No paid courses,
no app's built-in learning mentioned.

## Strongest signal

**Text steps stop working once the app gets complex.** For Photoshop they'd rather "see a
mouse on a screen moving" than read instructions. This is the second interviewee (after 003)
who uses a chatbot for their own project and YouTube for the visuals. Adobe's After Effects
assistant also answers in text. A mentor that only writes steps won't be enough for After
Effects, the most complex app on the list.

**Counter-signal on money:** they won't pay to learn, since AI and YouTube are free. If they
picture anyone paying, it's Adobe, as part of a bundle.

**New angle:** what makes them want to learn is **a look they want to copy** (a film look, a
specific photographer's style), not a list of skills. The saved-reels folder shows this is
something they actually do, not just an idea they agreed with.

## Notes on how I ran it

- Good: opened with what they'd actually done, and asked whether the ChatGPT learning carried
  over to later projects. That's a real behavior question.
- Leading: said "I do the same thing and the same problem" about saved reels, and my pitch
  described ChatGPT's and YouTube's weak spots before they reacted. "Brilliant" and "I would
  use it" are weak evidence. "Would it cost money?" and "I couldn't justify paying" are
  stronger.
- Missed: didn't ask whether they've tried Adobe's own AI assistant in Lightroom or Photoshop,
  or whether a record of their progress or structured practice would matter to them. Those
  are the exact questions `decisions/002` says Sprint 2 has to answer. Add them to the guide.
- Missed: didn't ask whether they'd want one tutor across Lightroom, Photoshop, and Final Cut.
  They're the best person so far to test the across-apps idea.
