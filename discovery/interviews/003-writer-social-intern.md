# Interview: writer and creative, former social-media content intern

**Date:** 2026-09-28
**How I found them:** in person, existing contact (recorded with a transcription app;
raw transcript not committed because it contains names)
**Persona:** not yet defined in `discovery/personas.md`

## What I asked / what happened

First interview using `bet-a-interview-guide.md`. Asked about past behavior first, then
pitched the idea partway through.

- Learned Premiere during an on-campus internship to make TikTok content that needed more
  than basic cuts ("I learned how to make things glow"). Had no video-editing background
  before that.
- Used **three sources together:** YouTube, ChatGPT and Gemini, and an in-house video editor
  for advice. "I couldn't have done it probably with one or the other."
- **YouTube:** "a very visual way of being able to follow someone else doing exactly what you
  want to do," but you "can't really adhere to exactly what you want to do." It's broader
  and "can be a little hard to translate" to your own project.
- **AI:** took screenshots when there was an error and asked "what am I looking at?"
  "Usually the AI could get it, but I already had the base understanding from the YouTube to
  be able to ask it."
- **Learn vs. just get it done:** "probably at the time it was more just to get the project
  done because we were on a crunch. And my specialty really isn't video editing." But the
  skill has stayed useful: they work with video editors now and occasionally do quick
  Premiere work themselves (transcribing, cutting down videos).
- **Skill decay:** "I don't know that I could do it now. I'm probably already rusty."
- **Premiere's built-in tutorials:** has used them. Helpful, but "not as much as what I was
  doing, which is why I was doing what I was doing." Brought up without prompting: integrate
  the built-in tips with your own project, "directly from there, but then show what it's
  talking about."
- **Reaction to the pitch:** "that's a good idea." Their comparison: "sort of a Khan Academy
  style thing, but more customizable." Asked whether it would work across all Adobe apps.
- On how big Adobe's apps are: "It's just so much. Like, it takes so long to learn
  sometimes." Even the experienced editor they know says there's a lot they don't know.

## What they actually do today

YouTube for the visual walkthrough, a general chatbot with screenshots to debug, and an
expert colleague as a backstop. Premiere's built-in learning was tried and set aside.

## Strongest signal

They already do a manual version of Bet A by hand: YouTube for the visuals and ChatGPT with
screenshots for their own project. Each covers the other's gap. So the real competitor is
that combination, not courses. To win, the product has to be **visual and project-specific
at once**.

The counter-signal matters just as much: **under deadline, a non-specialist wanted the
result, not the lesson.** The value of having learned it showed up later.

## Notes on how I ran it

- Good: opened with what they'd actually done and asked about each source separately.
- The pitch included a leading question ("I don't know if you felt this, but ... dumb
  practice projects") and my own claim that AI "isn't always super visual." Their agreement
  with those is weaker evidence than what they brought up themselves. Next time, ask
  "what was frustrating about tutorials?" before pitching.
